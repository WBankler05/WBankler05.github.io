Adaptive Shower Shield

An assistive device designed with, and for, one specific user.

Through Tikkun Olam Makers, my team developed an adjustable facial shower shield for a child with autism and sensory processing disorder. I designed and iterated the thermoplastic ear guard that made it adjustable and comfortable.

The challenge

Our need-knower, a child with autism and sensory processing disorder, found showering distressing because of water on the face. The goal was a facial shower shield that keeps water off the face while staying comfortable enough to actually be worn.

My role

  • Designed the ear guard that holds the shield in place, modeled it in SolidWorks, and fabricated it from thermoplastic filament.
  • Iterated the ear guard design to make the device adjustable and more comfortable.
  • Presented the group's work at a symposium of peers, faculty, and need-knowers.

Design process

Because the device had exactly one user, comfort and fit mattered more than anything else. I iterated the ear guard in thermoplastic until it could be adjusted to the user's head, which made the whole device more comfortable to wear.

Results

The adjustable thermoplastic ear guard improved user comfort, and both the need-knower and their caregiver gave positive feedback on the final device.
